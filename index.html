hi, i'm saanvi - first-year cs at uc berkeley, class of 2030, and i build things. that's honestly the shortest true sentence about me. i love love love the moment when something i made works in front of a real person: the glasses reading a room out loud, the model getting it right. that's the whole reason.

the assistive tech thing is personal. i've dealt with vision challenges myself, so i know exactly what it feels like when the world assumes you can see it. at bluestamp i built ai smart glasses - ultrasonic sensors, real-time object detection, voice controls, all on a raspberry pi - and the first time the prototype called out an obstacle on its own, i literally grinned like i couldn't stop. then came a cnn that reads chest x-rays and explains itself in plain language, a speech coach that took 1st place at future hacks, and signspeak, a real-time asl fingerspelling translator. same question every time: who is this hard for, and how do i make it easy?

i also just love shipping. at phyvant - an a16z speedrun-backed startup - i built and debugged the python evaluation harnesses that benchmarked their enterprise ai knowledge-graph platforms. hours of chasing one sneaky bug through a harness, then watching the whole suite go green? that's exactly my kind of joy. at personify i dug through feedback from 8,000+ users and helped push chatbot engagement up 9%. one system in front of real people beats ten notebooks on my laptop, every time.

when i'm not building, i'm on a basketball court or in a dance room. i played varsity - league champion 2023, ccs qualifier 2024, nationals winner 2022 - but the best part is coaching 120+ kids through project hoops and raising over $20,000 through tournaments and outreach. and i dance with naach, my bollywood fusion team: spinning, laughing at myself when i mess up, finally syncing with the music. the corner three you saw when this page loaded is not a stock animation. it's a scouting report.

the rest of my time goes to the communities that raised me: running an international humanitarian law campaign with the red cross (top 5 of 20, plus blood drives and a 500-person leadership camp), teaching python and java to 5,000+ students through kids teach tech, organizing isa events, working cal hacks logistics. teaching someone something i've just figured out - a kid a crossover, a younger dancer a step, a student their first loop - makes me feel like i'm spreading the spark.

if you're working on research, an internship, or a build where the technical details change someone's actual life - tell me. my email is at the bottom of this page and it goes straight to me.
